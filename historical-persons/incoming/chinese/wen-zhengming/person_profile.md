# Wen Zhengming / 文徵明 — Biographical Profile

## Identity

Wen Zhengming (文徵明, 1470–1559) was a Ming dynasty literatus, painter, calligrapher, and poet from Changzhou County, Suzhou Prefecture. Originally named Wen Bi (文壁), he later used Zhengming as his primary name with the courtesy name Zhengzhong (徵仲) and the art name Hengshan Jushi (衡山居士).

## Examination and Career

Unlike many of his peers, Wen Zhengming repeatedly failed the civil service examinations — both provincial and metropolitan. This prolonged struggle with the exam system shaped his early adulthood. In 1523, at the advanced age of 53, he was finally appointed to the Hanlin Academy as a Drafter (待诏) through the suigong (岁贡) route — a pathway for scholars who had repeatedly failed the exams.

His tenure at the Hanlin Academy was brief. He resigned within a few years and returned to Suzhou, where he spent the remaining three decades of his life as a professional artist and cultural leader.

## Artistic Achievement

Wen Zhengming was a versatile artist. In painting, he studied under Shen Zhou and developed a refined landscape style. His calligraphy was particularly renowned — his small regular script (小楷) and running script (行书) are considered among the finest of the Ming dynasty. He was also a prolific poet and essayist, with the *Futian Collection* (甫田集) preserving his literary output.

## Centrality in Suzhou Literati Networks

Wen Zhengming's extraordinary longevity (90 sui) made him a bridge across generations of Suzhou literati. He knew Shen Zhou as a teacher in his youth and outlived nearly all his contemporaries — Tang Yin (d. 1524), Zhu Yunming (d. 1527), Xu Zhenqing (d. 1510) — becoming the de facto elder of Suzhou cultural life. His associations with Qiu Ying and Wu Kuan further extended his network.

## Reception

Later art-historical tradition places Wen Zhengming among the "Four Masters of the Ming" (明四家), while literary tradition groups him among the "Four Talents of Wu" (吴中四才子). Both are retrospective classifications and should not be read as evidence of a formal group identity during his lifetime.
