# Xu Zhenqing / 徐祯卿 — Biographical Profile

## Identity

Xu Zhenqing (徐祯卿, 1479–1511), courtesy name Changgu (昌谷, also written Changguo 昌国), was a Ming dynasty poet and literary figure from Wu County, Suzhou Prefecture.

## Early Achievement and Early Death

Xu Zhenqing earned the jinshi degree in 1505 at the age of 26 — a significant achievement that distinguished him from several of his "Four Talents" peers who never attained the degree. However, his career and life were cut short: he died in 1511 at the age of only 33. This early death means his actual period of active participation in Suzhou literary circles was limited, and the extent of his personal relationships should not be overstated.

## Literary Output and Associations

Xu Zhenqing was primarily a poet. His collected works, the *Digong Collection* (迪功集), preserves his literary output. He also wrote the *Tan Yi Lu* (谈艺录), a work of literary criticism.

In literary history, Xu Zhenqing occupies an interesting dual position:

1. **Regional:** He is grouped among the "Four Talents of Wu" (吴中四才子) alongside Tang Yin, Zhu Yunming, and Wen Zhengming — a retrospective classification of Suzhou literary figures.

2. **National:** He is associated with the "Former Seven Masters" (前七子), a group of writers led by Li Mengyang (李梦阳) and He Jingming (何景明) who advocated archaist (复古) literary principles. Xu's association with this national literary movement connects the Suzhou regional literary scene to broader Ming literary currents.

## Caution on Relationship Claims

The "Four Talents of Wu" grouping is a later literary-historical classification and should not be interpreted as evidence that the four men formed a close-knit circle or maintained regular correspondence. Xu Zhenqing's early death further limits the plausible depth of these relationships. His association with Li Mengyang and the Former Seven Masters is better documented through literary-historical sources but also requires primary text verification.
