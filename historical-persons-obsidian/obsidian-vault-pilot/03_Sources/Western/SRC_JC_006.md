## Notes

Marcus Tullius Cicero (106–43 BCE) was Caesar's contemporary, political rival, and occasional admirer. His letters — especially the Letters to Atticus (Epistulae ad Atticum) and Letters to Friends (Epistulae ad Familiares) — provide the most immediate contemporary perspective on Caesar's political career.

**Source caveat:** Cicero was not neutral. As Caesar's political opponent (and later, after Pharsalus, a recipient of Caesar's clemency), Cicero's letters reflect his own shifting political stance. His praise for Caesar's clemency and literary style coexists with deep Republican opposition to Caesar's dictatorship.
