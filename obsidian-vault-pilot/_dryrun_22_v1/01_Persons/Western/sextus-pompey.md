# Sextus Pompey / 塞克斯图斯·庞培

## Summary

罗马共和国晚期军事指挥官。庞培大帝（格奈乌斯·庞培·马格努斯）的幼子。凯撒被刺后在西西里建立海军势力（前42–36年），控制罗马粮食供给，严重威胁后三头的统治。前36年在纳乌洛库斯海战中被奥古斯都部将阿格里帕击败。逃往东方，前35年在米利都被安东尼部将处决。古代文献在奥古斯都政权下编写，系统性地称其为「海盗」（praedo）——现代学术公认此为贬损性政治宣传。应使用「指挥官」或「海军指挥官」等中性术语。

Roman commander of the late Republic. Younger son of Pompey the Great. After Caesar's assassination, he established a naval power base in Sicily (42–36 BCE), controlling the grain supply to Rome and threatening the triumvirs. Defeated by Octavian's forces under Marcus Agrippa at the Battle of Naulochus (36 BCE). Fled to the East; executed in 35 BCE at Miletus on Mark Antony's orders. Ancient sources, written under the victorious Augustan regime, systematically label him a 'pirate' (praedo) — modern scholarship recognizes this as derogatory propaganda. Use 'commander' or 'naval commander' as neutral terms.

## Key Historical / Reception / Legend / Fiction Separation

### Confirmed Historical Facts
- Sextus Pompey was born ca. 67 BCE, the younger son of Pompey the Great (Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus) and his third wife Mucia Tertia.
- Sextus Pompey adopted the cognomen 'Pius' (the Dutiful), emphasizing filial loyalty to his deceased father Pompey the Great.
- After the Battle of Munda (45 BCE), Sextus Pompey escaped Caesar's forces and organized guerrilla resistance in Hispania.
- From 42 BCE, Sextus Pompey established a base in Sicily, controlling Sicily, Sardinia, and Corsica; his fleet blockaded Rome's grain supply.
- In 39 BCE, the triumvirs concluded the Pact of Misenum with Sextus Pompey, recognizing his control of Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, and the Peloponnese in exchange for lifting the grain blockade of Rome.
- On 3 September 36 BCE, Sextus Pompey's fleet was defeated by Octavian's admiral Marcus Agrippa at the Battle of Naulochus.

2. **Execution authority**: Was Sextus's execution at Miletus (35 BCE) ordered by Mark Antony, or did Marcus Titius act on his own initiative? Ancient sources disagree. The question bears on Antony's political strategy toward the Pompeian remnant.

3. **Pius coinage cataloguing**: A comprehensive die study and museum catalogue of Sextus's coin issues is needed for proper analysis of his self-presentation.

### Medium Priority

4. **Sextus's political program**: What was Sextus's positive political vision beyond resistance to the triumvirs? His adoption of the Pius title and Neptune imagery suggests a coherent ideology, but it must be reconstructed from material evidence (coins) and hostile literary sources.

5. **The Misenum negotiations (39 BCE)**: Details of the negotiations — who participated, what was promised, why the pact collapsed — need verification against multiple ancient accounts (Appian, Cassius Dio, Plutarch's passing references).

6. **Fleet composition and naval tactics**: Sextus's fleet was formidable enough to defeat Octavian repeatedly before Agrippa's reforms. What was the composition of his fleet, his tactical innovations, and his use of liburnians?
