# Pompey (Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus) / 庞培（格奈乌斯·庞培·马格努斯）

## Summary

罗马共和国晚期将领和政治家。原名格奈乌斯·庞培，获称号Magnus（“伟大的”）。三次凯旋者。剿灭海盗（公元前67年），击败米特里达梯六世（公元前66-62年），在西班牙与塞托里乌斯作战。与克拉苏共任执政官（公元前70年）。与凯撒和克拉苏结成后世所称的“前三头同盟”（公元前60年）。公元前52年任独任执政官。法萨卢斯战役（公元前48年）败于凯撒，在埃及被刺杀。建造庞培剧院——罗马第一座永久性石制剧院。

Roman general and statesman of the late Republic. Born Gnaeus Pompeius, earned the cognomen 'Magnus' (the Great). Three-time triumphator. Defeated pirates (67 BCE), Mithridates VI (66-62 BCE), and fought Sertorius in Hispania. Co-consul with Crassus (70 BCE). Formed the informal political alliance later termed the 'First Triumvirate' with Caesar and Crassus (60 BCE). Sole consul 52 BCE. Defeated by Caesar at Pharsalus (48 BCE) and assassinated in Egypt. Built the Theatre of Pompey, Rome's first permanent stone theater.

## Key Historical / Reception / Legend / Fiction Separation

### Confirmed Historical Facts
- Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus was born on 29 September 106 BCE in Picenum (modern Marche/Abruzzo region, Italy).
- His father was Gnaeus Pompeius Strabo, consul in 89 BCE, a wealthy landowner in Picenum and the first of his branch to reach senatorial status.
- Pompey raised and equipped a full legion from his father's former clients and veterans in Picenum for Sulla in 83 BCE during Sulla's civil war.
- In 82-81 BCE, Pompey defeated Marian allies in Sicily and Africa. He captured and executed Gnaeus Papirius Carbo in Sicily and defeated Gnaeus Domitius Ahenobarbus at the Battle of Utica in Africa.
- Pompey celebrated his first triumph in 81 BCE at approximately age 25 — unprecedented for someone so young and who had not yet held public office.
- Pompey issued coinage: a gold aureus for his second triumph (71 BCE) showing the head of Africa and Pompey in a triumphal chariot with his son Gnaeus and Victory; and a denarius (56 BCE) minted by Faustus Sulla showing three wreaths representing Pompey's three triumphs.

### Reception / Later Groupings (not formal organizations)
- His opponents called him 'adulescentulus carnifex' ('teenage butcher' / 'young executioner').
- Pompey's body was cremated by two faithful servants on the beach. His head was kept as evidence but later returned to his widow Cornelia, who buried it at Pompey's villa in the Alban Hills.
- According to ancient sources, when Caesar was presented with Pompey's severed head in Egypt, he wept — an episode widely circulated but potentially colored by Caesar's own propaganda and later literary embellishment.
- In later literary and historiographical tradition, Pompey has been portrayed as a tragic hero, a 'defender of the Republic,' and a foil to Caesar. Lucan's epic poem Pharsalia (1st century CE) is a key source for this reception image.
- Pompey's Pillar in Alexandria is a misattribution: it is actually a Roman triumphal column erected 298-302 CE in honor of Emperor Diocletian.
