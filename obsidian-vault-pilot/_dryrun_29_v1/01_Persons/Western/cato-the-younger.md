# Cato the Younger / 小加图

## Summary

罗马共和国末期元老、斯多葛派哲学家、贵族派领袖。以廉洁正直著称，终身反对凯撒，乌提卡战败后自杀殉国。

Late Roman Republic senator, Stoic philosopher, and Optimate leader. Renowned for incorruptibility and principled opposition to Julius Caesar. Committed suicide at Utica after the Pompeian defeat.

## Key Historical / Reception / Legend / Fiction Separation

### Confirmed Historical Facts
- Marcus Porcius Cato (Cato the Younger) was the great-grandson of Cato the Elder and a senator of the late Roman Republic.
- Cato was born in 95 BC and died at Utica in April 46 BC, aged approximately 49.
- During the Catilinarian conspiracy debate (December 63 BC), Cato successfully argued for execution of the conspirators against Caesar's proposal of life imprisonment.

### Disputed / Uncertain
- *(none flagged disputed)*

### Reception / Later Groupings (not formal organizations)
- Cato was immediately mythologized as a Stoic martyr and republican symbol. Cicero and Brutus each wrote Cato panegyrics (both lost). Caesar responded with Anti-Cato (lost).
- Cato became a symbol of republican virtue in Western tradition: Dante placed him as guardian of Purgatory, and George Washington staged Addison's Cato at Valley Forge (1777).
