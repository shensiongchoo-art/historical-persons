# Octavia Minor / 小屋大维娅

## Summary

罗马共和国晚期贵族女性。奥古斯都（屋大维）的同母姐姐。先嫁盖乌斯·克劳狄乌斯·马塞卢斯（前50年执政官），后因布伦迪西乌姆条约的政治需要嫁给马克·安东尼（前40年），成为安东尼与屋大维和解的人质纽带。前32年被安东尼为克娄巴特拉七世所弃。离婚后抚养安东尼与前妻富尔维娅及与克娄巴特拉所生的子女，同时抚养自己的子女。其子马塞卢斯曾为奥古斯都的假定继承人。奥古斯都为其建造奥克塔维娅柱廊（Porticus Octaviae），死后举行国葬。古代文献将其高度道德化为「贤德罗马妻子」并与克娄巴特拉形成对比——此叙述框架应视为接受史而非中立史实。

Roman noblewoman of the late Republic. Full sister of Augustus (Octavian) by the same mother, Atia Balba Caesonia. Married Gaius Claudius Marcellus (consul 50 BCE) and later Mark Antony (40 BCE) as part of the Treaty of Brundisium, sealing the political reconciliation between Antony and Octavian. Divorced by Antony in 32 BCE when he formally allied with Cleopatra VII. After the divorce, she raised Antony's children (including those by Fulvia and Cleopatra) alongside her own. Mother of Marcellus (Augustus's intended heir) and Antonia Major and Minor. Publicly honored by Augustus: the Porticus Octaviae and a state funeral with public mourning attest to her dynastic importance. Ancient sources heavily moralize her as the virtuous Roman wife contrasted with Cleopatra — treat this framing as reception.

## Key Historical / Reception / Legend / Fiction Separation

### Confirmed Historical Facts
- Octavia Minor was born ca. 69 BCE, daughter of Gaius Octavius and Atia Balba Caesonia, and was the full sister of Augustus (Octavian).
- Octavia first married Gaius Claudius Marcellus (consul 50 BCE), bearing one son (Marcellus) and two daughters (Claudia Marcella Major and Minor).
- In October 40 BCE, as part of the Treaty of Brundisium, Octavia married Mark Antony. The Senate granted a special dispensation waiving the required ten-month waiting period for remarriage.
- In 35 BCE, Augustus sent Octavia to Antony in the East with troops and supplies. Antony accepted the troops but ordered Octavia back to Rome — a public humiliation.
- In 32 BCE, Antony formally divorced Octavia, thereafter openly allied with Cleopatra VII.
- After the divorce, Octavia raised all of Antony's children — both by Fulvia and by Cleopatra — in her own household.

## Open Questions

### High Priority

1. **Exact birth year**: ca. 69 BCE is an estimate. Can prosopographical analysis of the Octavii family provide a more precise date? No ancient source records her birth.

2. **First marriage date**: Her marriage to Gaius Claudius Marcellus is undated in surviving sources. Was it before or after her father's death in 58 BCE?

3. **Porticus Octaviae vs Porticus Octavia**: These are two separate Augustan monuments. Exact archaeological identification and dating of each need clarification.

### Medium Priority

4. **Octavia's independent agency**: All surviving sources depict Octavia as acted upon by male figures (Octavian, Antony). Is there any evidence — epigraphic, numismatic, or literary — that hints at her independent political agency or preferences?

5. **The children**: A detailed prosopography of all the children Octavia raised (her own three from Marcellus, two by Antony, plus Antony's children by Fulvia and Cleopatra) would clarify her dynastic function.

6. **The Tarentum mediation (37 BCE)**: Ancient sources suggest Octavia brokered the renewal of the triumvirate between Antony and Octavian at Tarentum. How much of this mediating role is historical versus later idealization?

### Low Priority

7. **Octavia in Augustan poetry**: References to Octavia or her grief appear in Virgil (Aeneid VI.860–886, Marcellus passage), possibly Propertius and Horace. A systematic survey of poetic references is needed.

8. **Later reception in art**: Octavia's depiction in Renaissance and Baroque art (especially scenes of her departure or of the contrast with Cleopatra) should be catalogued.

9. **Numismatic survey**: A comprehensive catalogue of coins bearing Octavia's image with museum accession numbers.
