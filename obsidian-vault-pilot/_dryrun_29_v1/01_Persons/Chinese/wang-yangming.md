# Wang Yangming / 王阳明

## Summary

明代思想家、军事家、文学家。本名王守仁，字伯安，号阳明子，世称王阳明，谥文成，封新建伯（后进新建侯）。浙江余姚人。弘治十二年进士。官至南京兵部尚书。龙场悟道后发展心学，提出“知行合一”“致良知”等学说。平定宁王之乱，总督两广。后世视为阳明心学的核心奠基人物，从祀孔庙。著有《传习录》《大学问》等。其思想传入日本形成日本阳明学。

Ming dynasty philosopher, military commander, and writer. Original name Wang Shouren, courtesy name Bo'an, commonly known as Wang Yangming. Proposed doctrines of 'unity of knowledge and action' and 'extension of innate knowledge.' Suppressed the Prince of Ning rebellion. Later regarded as the central founding figure of the Yangming School of Mind. Canonized in the Confucian Temple (1584). Author of Chuanxilu (Instructions for Practical Living). His thought formed Japanese Ōyōmei-gaku.

## Key Historical / Reception / Legend / Fiction Separation

### Confirmed Historical Facts
- Wang Shouren (Wang Yangming) was born on the 30th day of the 9th month of the 8th year of the Chenghua reign (26 or 31 October 1472) at Ruiyun Tower, Yuyao County, Shaoxing Prefecture, Zhejiang Province.
- His original name was Wang Shouren.
- His courtesy name (zi) was Bo'an.
- His art name (hao) was Yangmingzi; commonly known as Wang Yangming.
- He was posthumously titled Wencheng (Completion of Culture).
- He was enfeoffed as Earl of Xinjian during his lifetime, posthumously promoted to Marquis of Xinjian.

### Disputed / Uncertain
- *(none flagged disputed)*

### Reception / Later Groupings (not formal organizations)
- Later historiographers classify Wang Yangming's philosophy as part of the 'Lu-Wang School of Mind,' viewing it as continuing the thought of Southern Song philosopher Lu Jiuyuan and standing in opposition to Zhu Xi's Cheng-Zhu School of Principle. This classification is a later historiographical construction.
- Scholars note that Wang Yangming's philosophy has a radical egalitarian implication: all people are equally capable of becoming a sage through the removal of selfish desires. This is a scholarly interpretation.
- In 1567, Wang Yangming was posthumously promoted to Marquis of Xinjian and given the posthumous title Wencheng (Completion of Culture).
- In 1584, Wang Yangming was offered sacrifice in the Confucian Temple (congsi kongmiao) — one of the highest honors for a Confucian scholar.
- Wang Yangming is often ranked in later Confucian historiography alongside Confucius, Mencius, and Zhu Xi among the most important figures in Chinese Confucian history.
- In later reception, Wang Yangming is often credited with achieving the 'True Three Immortalities' (establishing virtue, merit, and teaching), viewed as one of the rare figures in Chinese history who excelled in both civil and military affairs.

### Legendary / Fictional
- Legend holds that Wang Yangming did not speak until age five, beginning only after being renamed Shouren. His grandmother allegedly dreamed of a spirit delivering a child from the clouds, hence his childhood name Wang Yun.
