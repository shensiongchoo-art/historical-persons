# Tang Yin / 唐寅

## Summary

明代苏州文人、画家、诗人、书法家。1498年应天府乡试解元，后因科场案被黜。以绘画、诗词名世，后世常将其与沈周、文徵明、仇英并列于“明四家”，与祝允明、文徵明、徐祯卿合称“吴中四才子”。

Ming dynasty Suzhou literatus, painter, poet, and calligrapher. Placed first in the 1498 Yingtian provincial examination (jieyuan), but was implicated in an examination scandal and dismissed from official career. Known for painting and poetry; later art-historical tradition groups him among the 'Four Masters of the Ming' and the 'Four Talents of Wu'.

## Key Historical / Reception / Legend / Fiction Separation

### Confirmed Historical Facts
- Tang Yin was born in 1470 in Wu County, Suzhou Prefecture.
- Tang Yin died on the second day of the twelfth month of the second year of Jiajing (7 January 1524).
- Tang Yin, originally named Tang Yin, had the courtesy names Bohu and Ziwei, and used the art names Liuru Jushi and Taohua Anzhu.
- Tang Yin placed first (jieyuan) in the 1498 Yingtian provincial examination.
- In 1499, Tang Yin was implicated in a metropolitan examination scandal, imprisoned, and later degraded to clerk status.
- After the scandal, Tang Yin abandoned an official career and supported himself by selling paintings.

### Disputed / Uncertain
- The specific culpability in the examination scandal is disputed; whether Tang Yin accepted bribes or was merely implicated varies across historical records.

### Reception / Later Groupings (not formal organizations)
- In later art-historical tradition, Tang Yin is often grouped as one of the 'Four Masters of the Ming' alongside Shen Zhou, Wen Zhengming, and Qiu Ying.
- In later literary tradition, Tang Yin is grouped among the 'Four Talents of Wu' alongside Zhu Yunming, Wen Zhengming, and Xu Zhenqing.
- The story 'Tang Bohu Courted Qiuxiang' is a fictional or legendary tale from later literature and drama, and should not be treated as Tang Yin's actual biography.
