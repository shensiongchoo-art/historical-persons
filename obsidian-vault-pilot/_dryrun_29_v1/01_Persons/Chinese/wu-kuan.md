# Wu Kuan / 吴宽

## Summary

明代成化八年状元，官至礼部尚书。诗文与书法在苏州文人圈中有重要地位，后世常将其置于吴门文学与书法传统中讨论。与沈周、文徵明等苏州文人存在关联，但具体关系强度仍需更多原始材料核实。著有《匏翁家藏集》。

Ming dynasty zhuangyuan of 1472 who rose to Minister of Rites. Wu Kuan held an important place in the Suzhou literati world and is later discussed in relation to Wu School literary and calligraphic traditions. His associations with Shen Zhou, Wen Zhengming, and other Suzhou figures require further source verification for relationship strength. Author of Collected Works of Paoweng.

## Key Historical / Reception / Legend / Fiction Separation

### Confirmed Historical Facts
- Wu Kuan, courtesy name Yuanbo, art name Pao'an (also Paoweng), was from Changzhou, South Zhili (modern Suzhou, Jiangsu).
- Wu Kuan was born in 1435 and died on the 10th day of the 7th month of Hongzhi 17 (19 August 1504), aged 70 sui.
- In 1472, Wu Kuan placed first in both the metropolitan and palace examinations, becoming zhuangyuan (top jinshi).
- After achieving zhuangyuan, Wu Kuan was appointed Compiler in the Hanlin Academy and held various posts in the Crown Prince's instructional establishment.
- Wu Kuan became Vice Minister of Personnel (Right) in 1495 and Minister of Rites in 1503.
- Despite being the most prestigious literary attendant official of his era, Wu Kuan never attained Grand Secretary status.
