## Notes

Plutarch of Chaeronea (c. 46–120 CE) wrote the Parallel Lives pairing Caesar with Alexander the Great. Written approximately 150 years after Caesar's death, Plutarch drew on earlier sources that are now lost (including Asinius Pollio, Livy, and possibly Caesar's own lost works).

Key Loeb passages:
- Ch. 1–2: Birth and early life
- Ch. 32: Rubicon crossing
- Ch. 42–46: Pharsalus
- Ch. 59: Calendar reform
- Ch. 60–66: Assassination

**Source caveat:** Plutarch's aim was moral biography, not neutral history. His selection and framing of events reflect his interest in character and virtue. The Life of Caesar should be read alongside other sources (Suetonius, Cassius Dio, Cicero's letters) for a balanced picture.
