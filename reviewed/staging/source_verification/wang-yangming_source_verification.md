## Verified Citations

### Primary Texts (Existence Confirmed, but Passages not Cross-Referenced)

**《传习录》 (Chuanxilu)**: The core text of Wang Yangming's philosophy, compiled from his dialogues and letters. It exists in three parts:
- Part 1 (上): Compiled by Xu Ai (徐爱) — Wang's disciple and brother-in-law
- Part 2 (中): Letters, compiled by Wang's disciples
- Part 3 (下): Later dialogues, compiled posthumously
- Standard editions exist in 《王阳明全集》 (Shanghai Guji edition, 1992; multiple reprints)
- **Specific passage citations are needs_verification** — no paragraph/section/page references confirmed.

**《大学问》 (Daxuewen)**: A short work on the *Great Learning*, considered a mature statement of Wang's thought. Existence confirmed. **Specific edition/passage references needs_verification.**

**《明史·王守仁传》**: Located in 《明史》 (History of Ming), compiled by Zhang Tingyu (张廷玉) et al., completed 1739. The biography of Wang Shouren is in 卷195 (juan 195). This is the standard official biography. **Exact passage citations need cross-referencing.**

### Key Events (from Standard Scholarship)

- **Longchang (龙场) enlightenment (1508)**: Confirmed in Ming History and Wang's own writings. During exile as Guizhou post station chief, Wang realized the unity of knowledge and action (知行合一).
- **Suppression of Prince Ning (宁王) rebellion (1519)**: Confirmed in Ming History (卷195). Wang led military action against Zhu Chenhao's rebellion.
- **Four-Sentence Teaching (四句教)**: "无善无恶心之体，有善有恶意之动，知善知恶是良知，为善去恶是格物。" This formulation is documented in 《传习录》 but the specific passage reference needs_verification.
