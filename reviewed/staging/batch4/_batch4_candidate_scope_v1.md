## Context

Current staging repo has ~24 persons across Chinese (Ming literati/officials) and Western (Late Republic) networks. Batch 4 should strengthen the existing network rather than open new fronts.

**Existing Chinese network core**: Shen Zhou → Wen Zhengming → Tang Yin / Qiu Ying (Wu School art); Li Mengyang → He Jingming → Xu Zhenqing (Former Seven Masters); Wang Ao → Wu Kuan → Li Dongyang (Ming official-literati)

**Existing Western network core**: Caesar ↔ Pompey ↔ Crassus (triumvirate); Brutus ↔ Cassius (conspiracy); Cicero ↔ Cato (optimate opposition); Antony ↔ Octavian/Augustus (triumviral period)

## Candidate Evaluations

### 1. 王世贞 / Wang Shizhen (1526–1590)

**Why connects**: Leader of the Later Seven Masters (后七子), the literary movement that directly succeeded the Former Seven Masters (前七子, where Li Mengyang and Xu Zhenqing already sit). Wrote extensively on Ming history and institutions (弇山堂别集). Served as Minister of Justice in Nanjing. His Suzhou-area origins (太仓) connect to Wu School. His massive collected works (弇州山人四部稿) and library place him at the center of late-Ming literary culture.

**Existing related persons**: Li Mengyang (literary lineage, Former → Later Seven Masters), Xu Zhenqing, Wang Ao (Suzhou official-scholar network)

**Key events**: Jinshi 1547; clash with Zhang Juzheng; leadership of Later Seven Masters after Li Panlong's death; 弇州园 library; posthumous 太子少保

**Source availability**: STRONG. Zheng Lihua monograph 《王世贞研究》(2002), NPM exhibition catalog (2022, 21 contributors), own works (弇州山人四部稿, 弇山堂别集 — available on ctext.org), 明史 biography, CBDB entry (ID: 34717)

**Source risk**: **LOW**. Extensive primary writings plus modern monographs. Main challenge: not overstating literary-group relationships that were later historiographical constructs.

**Reception/legend risks**: "后七子" is a later grouping, not a formal organization. His rivalry with Zhang Juzheng needs balanced treatment. His namesake (Qing poet Wang Shizhen 王士禎) can cause confusion.

**v2.5 suitability**: Excellent. Primary text availability (A_candidate for own works), modern scholarship (B_high), 明史 (A_candidate).

**Recommended priority**: **HIGH**. Strongest Chinese candidate. Bridges Former → Later Seven Masters gap and expands the Ming literary-official network into the Wanli era.

### 2. 董其昌 / Dong Qichang (1555–1636)

**Why connects**: Most influential Ming painter-calligrapher-theorist after the Wu School generation. His "Southern School" (南宗) theory fundamentally shaped Chinese art history. Connects to Wen Zhengming and Qiu Ying as a later heir/perceiver of the Wu tradition. Jinshi 1589, high official. Represents the late-Ming scholar-amateur ideal.

**Existing related persons**: Wen Zhengming, Qiu Ying (art historical lineage — Dong theorized the tradition they practiced)

**Key events**: Jinshi 1589; development of Southern/Northern School theory; 民抄董宦 incident (1616 — his residence was burned by a mob; disputed historical episode); extensive painting and calligraphy output

**Source availability**: STRONG. Vast art historical literature. Major monographs and exhibition catalogs (Cahill, Ho Wai-kam, various). 明史 biography. His own theoretical writings (画禅室随笔).

**Source risk**: **LOW** for basic biography. HIGH for separating art-theoretical constructs from historical facts. The "Southern/Northern School" is his own historiographical invention, not an objective category.

**Reception/legend risks**: Very high. Dong's own theoretical framework has been naively accepted as fact by later tradition. The 1616 incident (民抄董宦) has competing interpretations. Reception risks require more work than typical packages.

**v2.5 suitability**: Good, but requires careful separation of Dong's own theories from claims presented as historical fact. Visual media staging is critical — his paintings are in museums worldwide but attribution debates are significant.

**Recommended priority**: **MEDIUM**. Important figure but:
- Period is later (1555–1636) — shifts the network window by a generation
- Direct person-to-person connections to existing figures are thin (Wu School artists were dead by his maturity)
- Complex art-theoretical apparatus needs heavy caveating
- **Better collected when we also add Chen Jiru (陈继儒) as a pair**

### 3. 陈淳 / Chen Chun (1483–1544)

**Why connects**: Direct student of Wen Zhengming (already collected). Wu School painter specializing in flower-and-bird subjects and cursive calligraphy. Represents the first generation of Wen Zhengming's disciples, strengthening the teacher-student network within the Wu School. Bridges Shen Zhou (grand-teacher) → Wen Zhengming (teacher) → Chen Chun (student) lineage.

**Existing related persons**: Wen Zhengming (teacher-student), Shen Zhou (grand-teacher, documented_association)

**Key events**: Studies under Wen Zhengming; develops independent flower-painting style; recognized as one of the key Wu School painters

**Source availability**: MODERATE. Museum records (Palace Museum, Shanghai Museum, Nanjing Museum), Chinese art history literature (明清画史, Cahill's Chinese Painting, 中国美术史). Less monograph-level coverage than Dong Qichang.

**Source risk**: **MEDIUM**. Fewer dedicated monographs. Personal life and personality less documented than his paintings. Most sources are art-historical, not biographical. Claims about his personality or relationships may need heavy caveating.

**Reception/legend risks**: Artwork attribution issues — as with all Ming painters, some works may be copies or misattributed. "Wu School" grouping is a later art-historical category.

**v2.5 suitability**: Adequate but constrained. Works catalog will be substantial (museum holdings). Personal claims will tend toward "probable" rather than "confirmed" due to thinner biographical sources.

**Recommended priority**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**. Strongest network logic of the three Chinese candidates (direct teacher-student link to Wen Zhengming). Source limitations are real but manageable with honest caveating. Strengthens Wu School core better than Dong Qichang does for this phase.

### 4. Cato the Younger (95–46 BCE)

**Why connects**: Uncle of Brutus (Batch 3). Leader of optimate opposition to Caesar. Stoic philosophical exemplar whose suicide at Utica became a symbol of Republican resistance. Connects to Brutus (uncle-nephew, philosophical mentor), Cicero (political ally, wrote "Cato"), Caesar (opponent), and Pompey (ally against Caesar).

**Existing related persons**: Brutus (nephew, philosophical disciple — Batch 3), Caesar (opponent — Batch 1), Cicero (ally — Batch 1), Pompey (ally — MVP)

**Key events**: Military tribune in Macedonia; quaestor in Cyprus; tribune 62 BCE; opposes Catilinarian executions; praetor 54 BCE; opposes First Triumvirate; commands Republican forces in Africa; suicide at Utica 46 BCE

**Source availability**: STRONG. Plutarch Life of Cato Minor (A_candidate), Cicero's "Cato" (lost but referenced), Cicero's letters and speeches, Sallust Bellum Catilinae, Lucan Pharsalia. Modern: Goodman & Soni "Rome's Last Citizen" (2012), Drogula "Cato the Younger: Life and Death at the End of the Roman Republic" (2019).

**Source risk**: **LOW**. Both ancient and modern sources are strong. Main risk: separating historical Cato from the Stoic exemplar / literary symbol he became. Plutarch writes moral biography; Lucan turns Cato into epic hero.

**Reception/legend risks**: High. Cato became a philosophical and political symbol almost immediately after his death (the "Cato legend"). Cicero's "Cato" praised him; Caesar's "Anticato" attacked him — both are advocacy, not neutral. Reception (Dante's Purgatorio, Addison's play, American Revolution Cato imagery) is rich but must be separated.

**v2.5 suitability**: Excellent. Well-documented ancient biography + strong modern monograph support. Legendary/reception material is fascinating but requires disciplined separation.

**Recommended priority**: **HIGH**. Strongest Western candidate. Completes the Brutus-Caesar-Cato triangle that is essential to the Late Republican network. Source quality is excellent.

### 5. Gaius Cassius Longinus (ca. 86–42 BCE)

**Why connects**: Co-leader of the conspiracy against Caesar with Brutus (Batch 3). Brother-in-law to Brutus (married Brutus' half-sister Junia Tertia). Key commander at Philippi. Connects to Brutus (co-conspirator, brother-in-law), Caesar (victim), Cicero (correspondent), Antony (opponent at Philippi).

**Existing related persons**: Brutus (co-conspirator, brother-in-law — Batch 3), Caesar (victim — Batch 1), Cicero (correspondent — Batch 1), Antony (opponent — MVP)

**Key events**: Quaestor under Crassus at Carrhae (survived); tribune; commanded Pompeian fleet; reconciled with Caesar; praetor peregrinus 44 BCE; organized conspiracy against Caesar; fled east after assassination; assembled army in Syria; First Battle of Philippi (his wing defeated by Antony, committed suicide believing Brutus had also lost)

**Source availability**: MODERATE. **No dedicated Plutarch Life.** Scattered across Plutarch's Brutus, Caesar, and Antony; Appian Civil Wars; Cassius Dio; Cicero Philippics and letters. No single ancient biography of Cassius — always a supporting figure.

**Source risk**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**. Always secondary in the sources. Personality, motives, early career are thinly documented. Harder to build a self-standing 16-claim package. Key facts about Carrhae survival and Philippi command are well-documented; personal details are sparse.

**Reception/legend risks**: Low, precisely because Cassius has been less mythologized than Brutus or Cato. Shakespeare's "lean and hungry look" (Julius Caesar I.ii) is the main literary reception element.

**v2.5 suitability**: Marginal. Can build a package, but claims will be thinner and more caveated than Brutus. Many claims will be "probable" rather than "confirmed." The package will necessarily be Brutus-centric in its relationship field.

**Recommended priority**: **HIGH-NECESSARY**. Despite source limitations, Cassius is **essential** to the conspiracy narrative. Without him, the Brutus package is incomplete. The Republican side at Philippi makes no sense without him. **Collect despite source risks**, with honest caveats.

### 6. Tiberius (42 BCE–37 CE)

**Why connects**: Step-son and successor of Augustus (MVP). Second Roman emperor. Connects to Augustus (step-son, adopted heir), Agrippa (Batch 1 — via Julia's marriages), and the Julio-Claudian dynastic network.

**Existing related persons**: Augustus (step-son, successor — MVP), Agrippa (forced to divorce Vipsania to marry Julia — Batch 1)

**Key events**: Born 42 BCE (same year as Philippi — historical irony); marriage to Vipsania Agrippina (Agrippa's daughter); forced divorce to marry Julia; retirement to Rhodes (6 BCE–2 CE); adoption by Augustus (4 CE); campaigns in Germany and Illyricum; accession (14 CE); withdrawal to Capri (26 CE); death (37 CE)

**Source availability**: STRONG. Suetonius Life of Tiberius, Tacitus Annals 1–6, Velleius Paterculus (contemporary, pro-Tiberius), Cassius Dio. Modern: Levick "Tiberius the Politician" (1976, rev. 1999), Seager "Tiberius" (1972, rev. 2005), Syme "The Augustan Aristocracy."

**Source risk**: **LOW** for documented facts. HIGH for character assessment — our main sources (Tacitus, Suetonius) are both hostile. Velleius offers a pro-Tiberius counterweight but is considered an apologist.

**Reception/legend risks**: Very high. Tiberius' reputation has been shaped by Tacitus' devastating portrait. His Capri years are subject to sensational accusations (Suetonius) that modern scholars question. "Tiberius the tyrant" is a literary trope that needs careful separation from historical assessment.

**v2.5 suitability**: Good. Well-documented. But larger issue: **Tiberius shifts the collection into the Imperial period.** The existing Western network is fully Late Republican (133–31 BCE). Tiberius (14–37 CE reign) opens an entire new era.

**Recommended priority**: **DEFER**. Not wrong to collect, but:
- Era shift from Late Republic to Empire is a significant scope expansion
- Thin connections to existing network (only Augustus and Agrippa)
- Would logically require Caligula, Claudius, Nero to follow
- **Better collected in a dedicated Julio-Claudian batch after Batch 5-6**

## Risk Notes for Collection

### 陈淳 source limitations
Museum records exist but a dedicated modern monograph may be hard to locate. Claim confidence will skew toward "probable." Prepared to accept a thinner package (12-14 claims rather than 16) if source evidence demands it.

### Cassius source limitations
No dedicated ancient biography means the package will be built from fragments across Plutarch, Appian, and Cicero. Claims about personality, early career, and motives will be caveated. Key facts (Carrhae, conspiracy, Philippi) are solid. This may be the thinnest v2.5 package in the repo — accept this as a known limitation.

### 王世贞 reception risks
"后七子" must be marked as reception/later_grouping_only. His literary polemics (attacking Song poetry, advocating Qin-Han prose) are his own advocacy positions, not neutral facts. His rivalry with Zhang Juzheng needs multi-source treatment.
