# Marcus Junius Brutus — Person Profile

## Summary

Marcus Junius Brutus (ca. 85–42 BCE), also known as Quintus Caepio Brutus after his adoption, was a Roman senator and the most famous leader of the assassination of Julius Caesar on the Ides of March (March 15), 44 BCE. He was a Stoic-influenced philosopher, a follower of his uncle Cato the Younger, and a respected figure in the late Republican optimate tradition. After Caesar's assassination, he fled to the eastern provinces with Cassius, raised an army, and was defeated by Mark Antony and Octavian at the Battle of Philippi, where he committed suicide.

## Key Roles

- Roman senator
- Praetor (44 BCE)
- Governor of Cisalpine Gaul (46–45 BCE)
- Leader of the conspiracy against Julius Caesar
- Commander of Republican forces at Philippi (42 BCE)

## Historical Significance

- His name became synonymous with political assassination in the name of Republican liberty
- Represents the senatorial opposition to Caesar's monarchical ambitions
- His philosophical writings (now lost) were admired by Cicero and Seneca
- The question of whether Brutus was Caesar's natural son is an enduring historical and literary motif
- Shakespeare's "Et tu, Brute?" has profoundly shaped later reception of both figures
