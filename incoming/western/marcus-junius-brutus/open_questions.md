# Open Questions — Marcus Junius Brutus

1. **Caesar's paternity**: Was Brutus Caesar's biological son? The rumor is chronologically improbable (Brutus born ca. 85 BCE when Caesar was ca. 15), but Caesar's long affair with Servilia keeps the question alive in reception.

2. **Brutus' philosophical works**: All of Brutus' philosophical and oratorical writings are lost. What was the nature and quality of his thought? Cicero's praise may be partly rhetorical and political.

3. **Motivations for the assassination**: Was Brutus primarily motivated by Republican ideology, personal ambition, family tradition (his ancestor L. Junius Brutus expelled the Tarquins), or complex personal reasons including his relationship with Caesar?

4. **Brutus as military commander**: How to evaluate Brutus' military competence? He won the first battle at Philippi but his overall strategic decisions are debated.

5. **Porcia's death**: The exact circumstances of Porcia's death remain uncertain. Which account — hot coals, charcoal fumes, or other method — is most historically plausible?

6. **Brutus and the eastern provinces**: Brutus' administration in the east (raising funds, building an army) needs more detailed analysis from epigraphic and numismatic evidence.

7. **Eid Mar coinage**: The full extent of Brutus' coinage program and its propaganda message. Were the coins intended primarily for troop payment or for wider circulation?

8. **Posthumous reputation**: How quickly did Brutus' reputation shift from tyrannicide-hero to cautionary tale? Tracing the reception from Augustan propaganda through Imperial literature needs further work.

9. **Relationship with Cassius**: The exact nature of the Brutus-Cassius partnership — Plutarch presents tensions between them, but how much is biographical dramatization?

10. **House of Junius Brutus**: Brutus' claimed descent from L. Junius Brutus (founder of the Republic) — how much does this family tradition explain his actions?
