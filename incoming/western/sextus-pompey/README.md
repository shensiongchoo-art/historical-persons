## Key Cautionary Notes

1. **"Pirate" label is Augustan propaganda**: Ancient sources (written under or after Augustus) consistently label Sextus Pompey a "pirate" (praedo). Modern scholarship recognizes this as derogatory propaganda that denied him legitimate political and military status. Use "commander" or "naval commander" instead.
2. **Pius cognomen**: Sextus adopted the cognomen "Pius" (the Dutiful) to emphasize filial loyalty to his father Pompey the Great's memory — a political self-representation.
3. **Sicilian base (42–36 BCE)**: Sextus's control of Sicily gave him leverage over Rome's grain supply, making him a serious threat to the triumvirs. His naval power was formidable.
4. **Source asymmetry**: Virtually all surviving accounts come from his enemies or their successors. Reconstructing Sextus's own perspective and motivations requires reading against the grain of hostile sources.
5. **Relationship types**: Do NOT infer "friendship" with his supporters or "alliance" with any Roman faction without explicit ancient attestation.
