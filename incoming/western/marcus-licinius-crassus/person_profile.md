# Marcus Licinius Crassus — Person Profile

## Summary

Marcus Licinius Crassus (ca. 115–53 BCE) was a Roman general, politician, and the wealthiest man in the late Roman Republic. He was a member of the First Triumvirate alongside Julius Caesar and Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus. Crassus is known for suppressing the Spartacus slave revolt (73–71 BCE), amassing an enormous fortune through real estate, silver mines, and slave trading, and for his disastrous invasion of Parthia that ended in his death at the Battle of Carrhae.

## Key Roles

- Consul (70 BCE, 55 BCE)
- Member of the First Triumvirate (60–53 BCE)
- Governor of Roman Syria (54–53 BCE)
- Commander against Spartacus (Third Servile War)

## Historical Significance

- Wealthiest Roman of his era — his fortune was legendary and became a byword for extreme wealth
- Defeated Spartacus and crucified 6,000 survivors along the Appian Way
- Formed the First Triumvirate with Caesar and Pompey, reshaping Roman politics
- His death at Carrhae destabilized the triumvirate and contributed to the civil war between Caesar and Pompey
