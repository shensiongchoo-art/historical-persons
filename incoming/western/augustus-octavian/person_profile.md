# Augustus (Gaius Octavius / Octavian / Imperator Caesar Augustus)

## 1. Objective Identity Summary

Augustus (63 BCE - 14 CE), born Gaius Octavius, was the founder of the Roman Principate and conventionally the first Roman emperor. After posthumous adoption by his great-uncle Julius Caesar in 44 BCE, he took the name Gaius Julius Caesar (called Octavian by modern historians for 44-27 BCE). Following the defeat of Mark Antony and Cleopatra at Actium (31 BCE), he consolidated power and in 27 BCE received the honorific Augustus from the Senate. His reign initiated the Pax Romana and established the political framework of the early Roman Empire. He was deified after his death. He composed the Res Gestae Divi Augusti and was patron to Virgil, Horace, and other Augustan literary figures.

## 3. Historical Existence Assessment

Augustus is one of the most thoroughly documented figures of classical antiquity. Sources include his own Res Gestae, Suetonius's biography, Cassius Dio's history, Appian, Velleius Paterculus, Cicero's letters and Philippics, extensive coinage, inscriptions, and archaeological remains. No serious doubt exists about his historicity.

## 5. Timeline Narrative

Gaius Octavius was born on 23 September 63 BCE to an equestrian family. His father governed Macedonia; his mother Atia was Julius Caesar's niece. When Caesar was assassinated in 44 BCE, his will posthumously adopted the 18-year-old Octavius as his son and primary heir. Taking the name Gaius Julius Caesar, he competed for the loyalty of Caesar's legions with Mark Antony. In 43 BCE he formed the Second Triumvirate with Antony and Lepidus, defeating Caesar's assassins Brutus and Cassius at Philippi (42 BCE). The Triumvirate divided the Roman world, but tensions escalated. After exiling Lepidus (36 BCE) and defeating Antony and Cleopatra at the Battle of Actium (31 BCE), Octavian became sole ruler of Rome. In 27 BCE, through a settlement with the Senate, he received the title Augustus and established the Principate. He expanded the Empire, reformed administration, and patronized the arts. The Varian disaster in Germania (9 CE) was a major setback. Augustus died on 19 August 14 CE, was deified by the Senate, and was succeeded by his adopted son Tiberius.
