# Gaius Cassius Longinus

**Person ID:** P_ROM_REP_CASSIUS_LONGINUS

## Summary

Gaius Cassius Longinus (c. 86-42 BC) was a Roman senator, general, and the primary instigator of Julius Caesar's assassination. An accomplished military commander who survived the Carrhae disaster and later defeated the Parthians in Syria, Cassius was the 'moving spirit' behind the plot against Caesar. An Epicurean philosopher, he developed a 'heroic Epicureanism' that justified political violence to preserve freedom. He committed suicide after the First Battle of Philippi.

## Key Facts

- Born: c. 86 BC, Rome
- Gens Cassia: ancient patrician family
- Quaestor under Crassus (53 BC); survived Carrhae
- Defeated Parthian invasion in Syria (51 BC)
- Praetor peregrinus (44 BC)
- Led Caesar's assassination (15 March 44 BC)
- Raised 12 legions in Syria (44-43 BC)
- Defeated at Philippi; suicide (3 October 42 BC)

## Philosophical Significance

Cassius's conversion to Epicureanism represents a 'conspicuous date in the history of Roman Epicureanism' (Momigliano 1941). His reconciliation of Epicurean withdrawal with active resistance to tyranny developed a distinctive 'heroic' interpretation of the philosophy.
