## Key Cautionary Notes

1. **Roman moralization**: Ancient sources (especially Plutarch) present Octavia through a moralizing lens — the virtuous Roman matrona contrasted with the "oriental seductress" Cleopatra. This framing must be treated as reception, not neutral history.
2. **Avoid "wronged wife" frame**: Octavia's divorce by Antony (32 BCE) is often narrated as a personal tragedy. The divorce was fundamentally a political act. Do not reduce her to the "abandoned wife" trope.
3. **Dynastic role**: Octavia's primary historical significance lies in her political-dynastic function as the link between Antony and Octavian (40–32 BCE), and as mother/stepmother to a generation of Julio-Claudian figures.
4. **Source asymmetry**: Octavia appears in ancient sources almost exclusively in relation to male figures (Octavian, Antony, Marcellus). Independent agency is difficult to recover.
5. **"Minor" designation**: Octavia Minor (the Younger) distinguishes her from Octavia Maior, elder half-sister of Augustus.
