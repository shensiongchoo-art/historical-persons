# Cato the Younger (Marcus Porcius Cato Uticensis)

**Person ID:** P_ROM_REP_CATO_MINOR

## Summary

Cato the Younger (95-46 BC) was a Roman senator, Stoic philosopher, and leader of the Optimate faction. Great-grandson of Cato the Elder, he was renowned for his incorruptibility and principled opposition to Julius Caesar. After the Pompeian defeat at Thapsus, he committed suicide at Utica rather than accept Caesar's pardon, becoming an enduring symbol of republican virtue.

## Key Facts

- Full name: Marcus Porcius Cato Uticensis
- Born: 95 BC, Rome
- Father died early; raised by uncle Marcus Livius Drusus
- Quaestor (65 BC): legendary reputation for honesty
- Tribune of Plebs (62 BC): expanded grain dole
- Annexed Cyprus (58 BC)
- Lifelong opponent of Julius Caesar
- Civil War: joined Pompey, retreated to Africa
- Suicide at Utica, April 46 BC
- Descendants: daughter Porcia married Brutus

## Legacy

Cato became an immediate martyr symbol. His legacy includes: Dante placing him in Purgatory, Addison's play *Cato* (1713), Washington staging it at Valley Forge, *Cato's Letters* inspiring American revolutionaries, and the modern Cato Institute.
