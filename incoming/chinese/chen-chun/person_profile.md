# 陈淳 (Chen Chun)

**Person ID:** P_CHN_MING_CHEN_CHUN

## Summary

Chen Chun (1483-1544) was a Ming dynasty painter, calligrapher, and poet from Suzhou. He studied calligraphy under Wen Zhengming but later broke away to develop his own expressive ink-wash (xieyi, 写意) style. He is particularly known for bird-and-flower painting and is paired with Xu Wei as one of the 'Baiyang and Qingteng' — the two great freehand flower masters of the Ming.

## Key Facts

- Courtesy name: 道复 (Daofu)
- Art name: 白阳山人 (Baiyang Shanren)
- Also known as: Chen Shun (variant romanization)
- Born: 1483, Suzhou, Jiangsu
- Studied under: Wen Zhengming
- Style: Freehand ink wash (水墨写意), influenced by Mi Fu
- Surviving works in: Freer Gallery, Cleveland Museum, Princeton, Metropolitan Museum, National Palace Museum Taipei
- Died: 1544

## Artistic Significance

Chen represents a pivotal transitional figure in Ming painting, bridging the more restrained Wu School literati tradition and the expressive individualism of later painters like Xu Wei. His 'Baiyang school' of flower painting is named after his art name.
