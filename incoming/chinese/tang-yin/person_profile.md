# Tang Yin / 唐寅 — Biographical Profile

## Identity

Tang Yin (唐寅, 1470–1524), courtesy name Bohu (伯虎), also Ziwei (子畏), was a Ming dynasty literatus, painter, poet, and calligrapher from Wu County, Suzhou Prefecture. In his later years he adopted the art name Liuru Jushi (六如居士, Layman of the Six Similes), referencing the Diamond Sutra. Other art names included Taohua Anzhu (桃花庵主).

## Early Life and Examination

Tang Yin showed literary talent early and associated with fellow Suzhou literati including Zhu Yunming. In 1498, he placed first (jieyuan) in the Yingtian provincial examination — a remarkable achievement that raised high expectations for his metropolitan examination.

## The Examination Scandal

In 1499, Tang Yin traveled to Beijing for the metropolitan examination. He and his friend Xu Jing (徐经) were accused of bribing the examiners' servants for questions. Both were imprisoned. Tang Yin was subsequently stripped of examination eligibility and demoted to minor clerk status — a punishment he refused to accept, returning to Suzhou in disgrace.

The exact nature of Tang Yin's involvement remains disputed in historical sources. Whether he was an active participant, an unwitting victim of his association with Xu Jing, or falsely accused by political rivals is unclear.

## Later Life and Career

After the scandal, Tang Yin abandoned any hope of an official career. He supported himself through painting sales, and traveled extensively through Jiangnan scenic sites. His painting incorporated both professional/academy techniques and literati sensibilities — a fusion that distinguished him from contemporaries.

He produced landscape paintings, figure paintings (especially of beautiful women), and bird-and-flower works. His poetry was noted for its unconventional spirit and direct emotional expression, often reflecting his personal disappointments.

## Reception and Legend

Tang Yin's historical reputation has been shaped by two overlapping traditions:

1. **Art-historical:** He is grouped among the "Four Masters of the Ming" (明四家) alongside Shen Zhou, Wen Zhengming, and Qiu Ying — a later art-historical classification.

2. **Literary:** He is listed among the "Four Talents of Wu" (吴中四才子) with Zhu Yunming, Wen Zhengming, and Xu Zhenqing — a later literary tradition.

3. **Popular:** The fictional story "Tang Bohu Courted Qiuxiang" (唐伯虎点秋香), in which he pursues a maid by selling himself into servitude, has no historical basis but has become the dominant popular image of Tang Yin through operas, novels, and modern media.

## Source Assessment

The primary sources on Tang Yin's life (明史·文苑传, 苏州府志) provide basic biographical framework, but specific passage references need verification. His collected works (六如居士全集) offer direct access to poetry and some biographical context. Modern scholarship provides art-historical analysis, but most claims about specific relationships and stylistic influences remain at the "probable" level pending primary source verification.
