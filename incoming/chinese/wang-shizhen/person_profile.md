# 王世贞 (Wang Shizhen)

**Person ID:** P_CHN_MING_WANG_SHIZHEN

## Summary

Wang Shizhen (1526-1590) was a Ming dynasty writer, historian, literary critic, and official. He co-led the Latter Seven Masters (后七子) literary movement with Li Panlong and, after Li's death in 1570, became the dominant literary arbiter of late 16th-century China.

## Key Facts

- Courtesy name: 元美 (Yuanmei)
- Art names: 凤洲 (Fengzhou), 弇州山人 (Yanzhou Shanren)
- Born: 1526, Taicang, Nanzhili (modern Suzhou, Jiangsu)
- Jinshi: 1547 (age 21)
- Father Wang Yu executed by Yan Song in 1560
- Led Latter Seven Masters with Li Panlong (d. 1570)
- Nanjing Minister of Justice, 1589-1590
- Died: 1590

## Major Works

- *Yanzhou Shanren Sibu Gao* (174 juan) — collected poetry and prose
- *Yanshantang Bieji* (100 juan) — unofficial Ming history
- *Jiajing Yilai Shoufu Zhuan* (8 juan) — Grand Secretary biographies
- *Mingfeng Ji* — play (authorship disputed)

## Literary Significance

Wang advocated archaizing (复古) literary theory: prose modeled on Han and earlier, poetry on High Tang. He later moderated his views. The Latter Seven Masters dominated Ming literary discourse from the 1540s through the 1580s. Wang's influence extended to Edo-period Japan.
