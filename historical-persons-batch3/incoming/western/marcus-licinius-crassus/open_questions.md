# Marcus Licinius Crassus — Open Questions

## Birth and Early Life

1. Exact birth year — 115 BCE is the conventional estimate based on Plutarch's relative chronology (Crass. 1.1) and career timeline. Can this be refined through prosopographical analysis?

2. Crassus's activities in Spain during the Marian years (87–83 BCE) — Plutarch's cave story may contain rhetorical embellishment. What can be verified about this period?

## Wealth

3. What was the real scale of Crassus's wealth relative to other late Republican aristocrats? Plutarch's figure of 7,100 talents needs contextualization against Pompey's and Lucullus's fortunes. The "richest man in Rome" label is a modern construction; can a more accurate comparative assessment be made?

4. The fire brigade/property acquisition story (Plut. Crass. 2.3-6) — was this a systematic business model or an exaggerated anecdote? What legal mechanisms existed for Crassus's property acquisitions?

## Political Power

5. How extensive was Crassus's political network of senatorial debtors and clients? Modern prosopographical studies could quantify his political influence beyond the triumvirate.

6. Crassus's role in the Catilinarian conspiracy (63 BCE) — Plutarch (Crass. 13.3-4) mentions accusations that Crassus was involved, which he survived politically. What sources support or refute this?

7. Did Crassus have a consistent political ideology, or was he purely pragmatic? Sources emphasize his wealth rather than his political convictions.

## Parthian Campaign

8. What were Crassus's actual military objectives in Parthia? Ancient sources emphasize glory and greed; modern military analysis (Sampson 2008) suggests more complex strategic considerations.

9. The exact troop composition and strength at Carrhae — Plutarch gives seven legions; Cassius Dio reports larger numbers. Can archaeological evidence or modern military modeling resolve the discrepancy?

10. The tactical dynamics of Carrhae — how exactly did Parthian horse archers and cataphracts coordinate to defeat Roman heavy infantry? Military historians continue to debate the tactical details.

11. The route of Crassus's march from Syria to Carrhae — does modern archaeological survey support the traditional reconstruction? The role of the Arab guide Ariamnes (or Abgar) as a Parthian agent needs critical examination.

## Death and Aftermath

12. The exact circumstances of Crassus's death — do the different accounts in Plutarch and Cassius Dio derive from a common lost source, or do they represent independent traditions?

13. What happened to the 10,000 Roman prisoners reportedly taken at Carrhae? Chinese sources and some Western traditions suggest they may have ended up as mercenaries or settlers in Central Asia. This claim needs rigorous examination.

## Historiography

14. How has the "Crassus as greed" moralizing tradition in Plutarch shaped modern scholarship? To what extent is the historical Crassus recoverable from the moral exemplum?
