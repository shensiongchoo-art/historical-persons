## Key Points

1. **One claim = one fact**: No compound claims. Each claim expresses a single verifiable fact.
2. **First Triumvirate is a reception label**: The 60 BCE alliance was an informal political arrangement, not a legally constituted office. The term is a later historiographical construction.
3. **Wealth characterization is calibrated**: Crassus's wealth is documented through ancient sources, but the 'richest man' label is a modern interpretive framing, not an ancient statistical ranking.
4. **Carrhae is documented with caution**: Ancient casualty figures are estimates based on source conventions. The 'molten gold' death story is marked as legendary.
5. **No verified portrait survives**: Unlike Caesar and Pompey, no coin or sculpture portrait of Crassus has been securely identified.

## Notable Biographical Highlights

- Fled to Spain and survived the Marian proscriptions (87–83 BCE)
- Suppressed the Spartacus revolt and crucified 6,000 rebels (71 BCE)
- Consul with Pompey (70 BCE, 55 BCE) — a career defined by rivalry
- Formed informal political alliance with Caesar and Pompey (60 BCE)
- Governor of Syria; invaded Parthia (54–53 BCE)
- Defeated and killed at the Battle of Carrhae (53 BCE)
