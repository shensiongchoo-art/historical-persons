# Marcus Junius Brutus — Open Questions

## Birth and Early Life

1. Exact birth year — 85 BCE is the conventional date based on Cicero's references (Brut. 324). Some scholars argue for 78 BCE based on Velleius Paterculus (2.72.1). Resolution requires systematic prosopographical analysis.

2. Brutus's relationship with his father — Marcus Junius Brutus the Elder was killed by Pompey in 77 BCE after the revolt of Lepidus. Did this affect Brutus's later decision to side with Pompey against Caesar? Plutarch says Brutus refused to speak to Pompey for years (Brut. 4.1-2).

## Political and Military Questions

3. Why did Brutus join Pompey's side in the civil war (49 BCE)? Ancient sources give multiple explanations (anti-tyranny principle, family tradition, Cato's influence). Can Brutus's own motivations be reconstructed from his actions and writings?

4. What was the nature of Brutus's relationship with Caesar after the pardon (48–44 BCE)? Caesar promoted Brutus to senior offices, but was their relationship genuinely reconciled or politically expedient?

5. The exact sequence of Brutus's decision to join the conspiracy — Plutarch's timeline may be compressed. When did Cassius first approach Brutus, and what arguments persuaded him?

6. Brutus's political strategy after the assassination — why did he oppose Cicero's push for aggressive action against Antony? The letters (ad Brut.) show Brutus urged restraint. Was this a strategic error or principled position?

7. The constitutional vision of the conspirators — did Brutus and Cassius have a plan for post-Caesar governance beyond removing the dictator? The evidence is fragmentary.

## Military Questions

8. The troop strength of the Liberator army at Philippi — Appian and Plutarch give differing figures. Can modern scholarship reconcile the estimates?

9. Was Brutus pressured into the second engagement at Philippi by his officers, or did he make a genuine strategic decision? Plutarch (Brut. 48-50) emphasizes the pressure from his commanders.

## Sources and Historiography

10. How much of Brutus's philosophical and political thinking can be reconstructed from the surviving fragments of his works? The loss of his philosophical treatises is a major gap in the source record.

11. The authenticity of the Epistulae ad Brutum — which letters in the Cicero-Brutus correspondence are genuinely contemporary, and which may be later rhetorical exercises? This is a longstanding scholarly debate.

12. How did Nicolaus of Damascus, writing under Augustus's patronage, shape the surviving narrative of the assassination? His account (FGrH 90 F130) contains details not found in later sources.

## Reception

13. When did the rehabilitation of Brutus's reputation begin in Roman literature? Tacitus (Ann. 4.34-35) records that Cremutius Cordus was prosecuted under Tiberius for praising Brutus and Cassius in his history.

14. How did the discrepancy between Brutus's EID MAR coinage (celebrating the assassination) and the "noble, reluctant tyrannicide" of Plutarch's biography develop in the source tradition?

15. The Porcia wounding and suicide stories — how much of the Porcia narrative in Plutarch is historical versus dramatic embellishment? The "swallowing hot coals" suicide method is physiologically implausible and may be a later invention.
