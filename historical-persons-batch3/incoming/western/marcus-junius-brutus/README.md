## Key Points

1. **One claim = one fact**: No compound claims. Each claim expresses a single verifiable fact.
2. **Shakespeare is marked as reception, not history**: 'Et tu, Brute?' is a Shakespearean invention (1599), not found in ancient sources. Shakespeare's Brutus is a literary character, not historical evidence.
3. **Motives are carefully marked**: Brutus's political and personal motivations for the assassination are complex. Claims about his ideology or beliefs are marked as interpretation unless directly sourced to his own writings or contemporary testimony.
4. **The 'Noble Brutus' tradition is reception**: The characterization of Brutus as a purely noble, selfless tyrannicide is a literary and moralizing construct from Plutarch and later tradition.
5. **The ghost story is legendary**: The phantom appearing to Brutus before Philippi is a literary/moralizing element, not a verifiable historical event.

## Notable Biographical Highlights

- Mother Servilia was Caesar's longtime mistress
- Sided with Pompey against Caesar in the Civil War (49–48 BCE), then pardoned
- Served as urban praetor under Caesar (44 BCE)
- Leading conspirator in Caesar's assassination (Ides of March, 44 BCE)
- Raised forces in the East with Cassius (44–42 BCE)
- Defeated by Antony and Octavian at Philippi (42 BCE); committed suicide
