# Zhu Yunming / 祝允明 — Biographical Profile

## Identity

Zhu Yunming (祝允明, 1461–1527), courtesy name Xizhe (希哲), art name Zhishan (枝山), also known as Zhizhi Sheng (枝指生, "the student with an extra finger"), was a Ming dynasty calligrapher, poet, and literary figure from Changzhou County, Suzhou Prefecture.

## Family Background

Zhu Yunming came from a distinguished scholarly lineage. His grandfather Zhu Hao (祝颢) was a jinshi degree holder, and his maternal grandfather Xu Youzhen (徐有贞) was a prominent court official during the Tianshun reign. This family background provided him with exceptional early education and cultural capital.

## Career

Zhu Yunming passed the provincial examination (juren) in 1492 but did not advance further in the examination system. He served in several minor official positions, most notably as magistrate of Xingning County in Guangdong and later as Vice-Prefect (通判) of Yingtian Prefecture. His official career was relatively modest compared to his literary and artistic reputation.

## Calligraphic Achievement

Zhu Yunming is best known for his cursive script (草书) calligraphy, which is characterized by bold, free-flowing brushwork. Alongside Wen Zhengming and Wang Chong (王宠), he is considered one of the great calligraphers of the mid-Ming period. His transcriptions of classical texts such as Su Shi's "Red Cliff Rhapsody" (赤壁赋) and Cao Zhi's "Goddess of the Luo River" (洛神赋) in cursive script are among his most celebrated works.

## Literary Output

His collected writings, the *Huaixingtang Collection* (怀星堂集), preserves his poetry and prose. He was also grouped among the "Four Talents of Wu" (吴中四才子) alongside Tang Yin, Wen Zhengming, and Xu Zhenqing — a later literary-historical classification.

## Folk Reception

The historical Zhu Yunming should be distinguished from the folk character "Zhu Zhishan" popularized in later fiction and drama, particularly stories set in the Jiangnan region. This comedic, quick-witted folk persona bears limited resemblance to the historical scholar-official and calligrapher.
